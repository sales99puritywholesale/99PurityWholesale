# Break-glass procedure for owner recovery (rev5): when every in-system path is exhausted

Status 2026-11-10. **SPECIFICATION, not an adopted procedure; nothing here was executed.** It needs the project owner's adoption and signature (see `docs/P2-IDENTITY-VERIFICATION-POLICY-SPEC.md` section 2). The database (0005 rev5) is **not applied**; this procedure describes how an emergency would be handled once it is.

## 1. When it applies (and when it does not)
Applies only to an organization **owner** in the defined failure state **stranded** (`rpc_my_owner_context.recovery_status = 'stranded'`, or listed as `stranded` by `rpc_staff_list_owner_factor_loss`): the owner's authenticator factors were removed under an attested recovery and the request ended (cancelled, expired, or the windows ran out) without an approved new factor, AND the in-system paths below are blocked or unacceptable for a documented business reason. It also applies to `unexplained` entries of the factor-loss review once the lead has concluded that the owner is legitimate and stranded.
**Not break-glass:** a normal lost authenticator (use recovery codes or the operator-initiated process), a first enrolment, anything for **staff** (staff recover through another administrator, `docs/P2-2-ADMIN-BOOTSTRAP-PROCEDURE.md`), and any case where the owner's identity is in doubt (that case is rejected and escalated, policy section 6).

## 2. In-system paths to try first (each keeps every guarantee)
**Rev6 wording (supersedes the numbers below where they differ):** a request has **three standard windows** plus at most **two explicitly authorized extensions** (`rpc_staff_authorize_extra_recovery_window`, five in total), within its 7-day validity; the factor removal is verified per window by the database (`rpc_staff_begin_factor_removal` and `rpc_staff_complete_factor_removal`); a **stranded-owner exception** (two operators) lifts only the per-owner quota for one new request.
1. **Continue the same request.** After a verified removal a request may issue up to 3 standard recovery windows and 2 explicit extensions (60 minutes each at most, never beyond the request's expiry) within its 7-day validity. An operator opens another window and the owner enrols a new factor.
2. **Another owner of the organization** (if there is one) carries on business meanwhile; owner capabilities are per person.
3. **A new request.** Owner-code request (if the owner still has a recovery code; 24 hour cool-off, no lock applies after a factor removal) or a new operator-initiated request (two different operators, independent evidence, 24 hour cool-off after the second approval). Quota: 5 initiations per operator per 24 hours and **2 per owner per 7 days**; **no exemption exists for a stranded owner**.
4. **Wait.** The owner keeps password sign-in and ordinary member access; only owner-level, MFA-gated actions are unavailable. Waiting for the 7-day quota window to pass is the default when nothing is urgent.

## 3. What break-glass may and may not do
**Never, under any authority:** create, approve or insert an approved factor for an owner (the approval trigger needs a verified TOTP factor that the owner created inside a window, and only the owner can do that); write to or delete from `auth.*` outside the Dashboard removal that the attestation records; delete, edit or hide request, window, approval or audit rows; disable a trigger; satisfy owner MFA or step-up by any means other than the owner's own TOTP; shorten the 24 hour cool-off; use fewer than two different people.
**Rev6 (approved decisions 3, 5 and 7): break-glass has NO code path and NO database effect.** The emergency-allowance migration that rev5 anticipated is **not needed and is not planned**: the in-system **stranded-owner exception** (two different active operators, each with fresh step-up; the database confirms the stranded state at request, grant and consume; it lifts only the per-owner quota of ONE new request, at most 2 per owner per 30 days) and the **explicit window extensions** (at most two beyond the three standard windows, five in total, each audited) cover the real cases inside the system, with every guarantee intact (two-person approval, 24 hour cool-off, the verified factor removal, MFA enrolment by the owner). What remains of break-glass is **only a recorded human decision** (which in-system path to use, or to wait). It cannot create or approve a factor, satisfy owner MFA or step-up, shorten the cool-off, change a quota or a counter, or touch any recovery table; any future need beyond this is a new, separately reviewed SQL revision.

## 4. Roles and records
Decision by **two different humans** who did not act as initiator or approver on the failed case: the designated security/operations lead and the project owner. Each decision is recorded in the case system (case reference, owner id, reason in operational terms with no personal data, the path chosen, who decided, when). The monthly review (policy section 9) lists every use. A use of break-glass is an incident: preserve records, review all operator actions on the case, report to the project owner.
An emergency migration, if one is ever approved, writes its own audit event through the audit function and keeps the append-only history; the audit table stays append-only and no row is removed.

## 5. Why this is acceptable
The residual risk is availability for a legitimate owner for up to a week in a rare failure, traded against never letting any person (operator, lead or database administrator) create trust in a factor on the owner's behalf. Reducing that wait is a product decision with a security cost and belongs to the project owner (open decision, `docs/P2-4-0005-REV5-SECURITY-REVIEW.md` section 7).
