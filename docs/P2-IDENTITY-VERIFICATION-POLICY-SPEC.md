# Identity-verification policy: specification (for operator-initiated owner recovery)

**Revision 3 (2026-11-10, aligned with the 0005 rev6 DRAFT; nothing is applied).** Changes: the factor removal is now ordered, performed manually and **verified by the platform per window** (section 5 steps 7 and 8; no typed count, no attestation by statement); three standard recovery windows plus at most two explicit extensions; the stranded-owner exception (two operators); break-glass has no code path and no MFA effect (`P2-4-RECOVERY-BREAK-GLASS.md`). **No legal retention period is stated or implied anywhere in this document: the figures in section 7 remain placeholders that need legal and privacy advice, and nothing here claims compliance with any law.**
**Revision 2 (2026-11-10, aligned with 0005 rev5; superseded in part by revision 3 above).** Changes: a notarized statement is a supplementary flag, never a method (section 4); the procedure follows the R1 sequence, in which the owner's factor stays in place until a recovery window is open and the factor removal is attested (section 5); how one human is prevented from acting as both operators (section 3.2); retention and erasure controls (section 7); break-glass reference (section 6).

Status 2026-11-10. **SPECIFICATION, not yet an adopted policy.** It turns decision D-V7 into a document that the project owner can adopt, amend and sign. The database (0005 rev5, not applied) records and enforces only what it can: which two accounts acted, which method and case reference each recorded, the cool-off, the quotas and the audit trail. **The quality of the identity verification itself is a procedure; this document is that procedure.** Items marked **[PROPOSAL]** are defaults I propose and that need the owner's confirmation (and, for retention and privacy, legal advice); none of them is a fact about the law.

## 1. Purpose and scope
Used when an organization **owner** has lost **both** their authenticator and their recovery codes and asks to regain owner privileges. It is also the template for any future operator-verified identity step. It never applies to **staff** (staff recover through another administrator and the break-glass procedure), and it never changes an owner's role: it only allows a **new factor to be enrolled** after a two-person authorization, a 24-hour cool-off and an operator-opened enrolment window.

## 3. Principles
1. **No direct MFA bypass.** A successful verification leads only to an operator-authorized enrolment window; the owner still enrols a new factor inside it and proves it with a fresh TOTP.
2. **Two different people.** The person who **initiates** a case and the person who **approves** it are different human beings, using their own personal accounts. Shared accounts are forbidden; one person must never hold two operator accounts; a person must not approve a case they initiated, nor verify anyone they have a personal or financial interest in (declare the conflict and hand the case on). The database enforces different **accounts**; this rule makes them different **people**.
3. **Independent evidence.** The approver performs their **own** verification, using their own method and reference, and does not rely on the initiator's evidence. The two references differ. A notarized statement is **supplementary only** (rev5: a flag next to a real method, never a method itself, so the platform cannot even record it as anyone's only evidence): each operator must complete one of the three real methods.
4. **The request channel is not proof.** A request arriving by email, chat or phone is a claim, never evidence. The account's own mailbox is never sufficient by itself (a compromised mailbox is the attacker's best tool).
5. **Slow and visible.** 24-hour cool-off after the second approval; the owner is notified through the application and through every independent contact on file and can cancel; every step is audited.
6. **Data minimization.** Record what is needed to show that verification happened; keep identity documents and biometric data out of the platform entirely.

### 3.2 Preventing one human from acting as both operators through separate accounts (organizational controls; the database cannot see people)
The database distinguishes **accounts** only. These controls make the two accounts two people; the monthly review (section 9) checks them.
1. **One natural person, one operator account.** Operator accounts are created only by the security/operations lead against the staff roster (full legal name, employee or contractor id); no shared, generic or role accounts; the lead records the person-to-account mapping in the case system and re-confirms it every quarter. A person holding two operator accounts is a policy violation and an incident.
2. **One authenticator per person.** Each operator enrols their **own** authenticator device themselves (the platform approves the factor inside an operator-authorized window; the person who authorizes a window for an operator is never that operator). Sharing a device, a seed or a screen during a step-up is forbidden and covered by the yearly attestation.
3. **Attestation.** Each operator signs once a year (and at onboarding): "I hold exactly one operator account, I never act under another person's account, and I never approve a case I initiated." The signed statements are kept with the policy.
4. **Case-system separation.** The case system (where evidence and notes live) uses its own authentication; the approver opens the initiator's record in their own session and records their own verification separately. A case record whose two verification entries were created from the same session, device or network location within the same few minutes is flagged.
5. **Monthly cross-check (the detective control).** For every initiate/approve pair in the audit table (`staff.owner_recovery.initiate` and `.approve`, with the operator ids and timestamps) the lead checks: two named people, two distinct case-system sessions, a plausible time gap that matches two separate verifications, no repeated identical pairs that always approve within minutes, and no operator whose approvals are all for the same initiators. Sign-in locations and device records for the operator accounts (platform sign-in logs, retained as long as the platform keeps them) are compared. Exceptions go to the project owner.
6. **Separation of duty on the removal.** The operator who orders and completes the removal is any capable operator; the lead reviews every completed order together with `rpc_staff_list_owner_factor_loss` (factor loss without a completed order is a finding) and every use of an exception or an extension (their audit events name both operators).
7. **Residual risk, stated:** two colluding people, or one person who controls two real identities and two devices, are not detectable by the platform. The controls above reduce and detect; they do not eliminate.

## 4. Accepted methods and their conditions
| Method (recorded value) | Status | Conditions that must all hold |
|---|---|---|
| **In-person verification** (`in_person`) | accepted | a trained verifier meets the person; checks a valid government photo ID against the person and against the name registered for the owner role; confirms authority (the person is the registered owner, or holds documented authority); **does not copy or photograph the ID**; records only the case reference and the outcome |
| **Verified business contact** (`verified_business_contact`) | accepted **with independent-channel verification** | contact details come from a source **independent of the request and of the account** (the contract or onboarding file, an official business register, the organization's published number or domain verified separately), never from the requester; the verifier reaches the person through that channel (call-back or message to the registered domain), confirms identity questions that were agreed at onboarding or that only the owner can answer, and confirms the owner role and the request; the channel used is recorded by type in the case file, not in the platform |
| **Video ID check** (`video_id_check`) | accepted **only if** all of: (a) a **documented procedure** exists and is followed (live session started by the verifier on an approved platform, scheduled through an independent channel); (b) the verifier is **trained and certified** (document tampering, deepfake and injection-attack awareness); (c) **liveness checks** are performed (unpredictable challenge-response such as random gestures or phrases, camera and lighting checks, comparison of live face with the photo ID shown on camera); (d) **secure evidence handling** (section 7): no screenshots or recordings outside the approved case system, nothing in chat or email |
| **Notarized statement** (recorded only as the supplementary flag `notarized_supplement` / `second_notarized_supplement`, never as a method) | **supplementary evidence only** | never sufficient by itself; counts only next to the operator's own completed standalone method (the operator must record one of the three methods above); checked for authenticity with the issuing notary through an independent channel; the notary's details stay in the case file |
Methods not on this list are not accepted. Adding one needs a new policy version and a new SQL revision.

## 5. Procedure for one case
1. **Intake.** The request is logged in the case system and given a **case reference** (format in section 8). Triage against the red flags in section 6. The requester is told only the generic process, never whether an account exists.
2. **Initiation (operator 1).** Verifies identity and authority with an accepted method (section 4), notes the evidence in the case file, then initiates the request in the platform with the **method** and the **case reference** and a short operational reason (no personal data). The case now **awaits the second operator** (72-hour deadline).
3. **Approval (operator 2).** A different person repeats the verification **independently** (different evidence, own reference and method), checks the first operator's record for consistency and red flags, then approves in the platform. The **24-hour cool-off starts at this approval.**
4. **Notification.** The application notifies the owner (email to the address on file) and, where an independent contact exists, through it. The notice says what was requested, when, and how to cancel; it contains no personal data beyond what the owner already knows. The owner can cancel at once after signing in. Any owner cancellation locks new requests for 24 hours and **must be escalated** (section 6).
5. **Cool-off.** Nothing happens for 24 hours. Either operator may cancel (reason required) if new doubts arise.
6. **Window first (rev5, R1).** After the cool-off, and **while the owner's existing factor is still in place**, an operator opens the recovery enrolment window (5 to 60 minutes, one use, at most three windows per request, six after an attested removal). The operator arranges a live session with the owner for that hour (video or call through the independent channel) so that the steps below happen back to back.
7. **Factor removal, inside the window (rev6: order, manual removal, database-verified completion).** First the operator **orders** the removal in the platform (`rpc_staff_begin_factor_removal`): the platform reads and shows the **exact factor ids** of that owner; the operator never types ids or counts. The operator then removes **exactly those factors, of that owner only,** by hand in the Supabase Dashboard (Authentication, Users, the user, factors; compare the user id in the case file and the factor ids from the order with the screen **before** pressing delete; a second operator watches) and signs out the user's sessions. The operator then **completes** the order (`rpc_staff_complete_factor_removal`, which takes only the order id): the **platform reads the factor state itself** and completes only if the owner has no verified factor left; if one remains the completion is refused (at most 5 tries) and a factor the order did not list makes the order stale (a new order is needed). The platform verifies the OUTCOME only: it does not know who deleted what or through which screen; collateral deletion of another user's factors is **detected afterwards** by the lead's review of `rpc_staff_list_owner_factor_loss`, not prevented. Never remove factors **before** ordering (the window cannot then be verified and is wasted). If the platform's check of the Auth structure is not fresh it refuses everything (`unknown_state`): ask a staff colleague to refresh it, never work around it.
8. **Enrolment.** Only after the platform verified the removal does the owner enrol a new authenticator **inside** the window, verify the first code and claim it; a new set of recovery codes is issued afterwards. If the enrolment fails (device problem, no-show) the removal stays on record, the owner state is recovery-required, and the operator opens another window (**three standard windows per request, plus at most two explicitly authorized extensions, five in total**; each extension needs fresh step-up and an audited reason); a leftover factor of an earlier window needs its own ordered and verified removal in the next window. A request that ends without a new factor leaves the owner **stranded** (section 6): two different operators can then request and grant a **stranded-owner exception** (30 day lookback, at most two per owner per 30 days, each with fresh step-up) which lifts only the per-owner quota of one new request; everything else (two-person approval, 24 hour cool-off, verified removal, MFA enrolment) applies again in full.
9. **Closure.** The operator checks that the case completed (request status `completed`, owner able to sign in with the new factor), closes the case file, and confirms the notification of completion. Failed or abandoned cases are closed with the outcome.

## 6. Rejection and escalation
**Reject (cancel the request, record the reason) when:** identity or authority cannot be confirmed through an independent channel; evidence is inconsistent between operators; the requester pressures for speed, refuses the independent channel, or asks to skip steps; someone other than the owner asks on the owner's behalf without documented authority; the account shows signs of recent takeover (password changed, new email, odd sign-ins); the requester cannot answer onboarding questions; the same person asks repeatedly from changing contacts.
**Escalate to the security/operations lead immediately when:** the owner cancels a request the operators believed genuine; two attempts against one owner occur within 7 days (the platform limits initiations per owner); a verifier suspects deepfake or document fraud; an operator is asked to bypass a step; any evidence of insider involvement. The lead decides on freezing the account, informs the project owner, and starts the incident process (preserve records, notify affected parties as required, review all recent operator actions). **Insider suspicion is never handled by the same two operators.**
**Stranded owner (rev5).** If an owner's factors were removed under an attested recovery and the request ended without an approved new factor, the platform reports the state `stranded`. The owner may use a recovery code (if any remain) or the operators may start a new two-person request (24 hour cool-off, the 2-per-owner-per-7-days quota still applies; the owner's own cancellation lock no longer applies once the factor is gone); if urgent, the break-glass procedure (`docs/P2-4-RECOVERY-BREAK-GLASS.md`) applies. **An owner's cancellation of a request after the factor was removed is not escalated as hostile by itself; before removal it still is.**

## 7. Privacy, evidence handling and retention
1. **Never in the platform:** identity documents, photographs, biometric data, dates of birth, document numbers, notary details, screenshots, recordings. **Never in audit logs, tickets' free text, chat, email or the references.** The platform stores only: operator ids, method names, case references, timestamps, status. Free-text reasons are kept short, operational and free of personal data (they are stored in the case rows and are not shown to the owner).
2. **Evidence, if any is retained at all,** lives only in the approved case system: encrypted at rest and in transit, access limited to the designated lead and the verifiers of that case, every access logged, no local copies, no forwarding. Prefer **not retaining** evidence: a verifier notes what was checked and the outcome.
3. **Retention [PROPOSAL, needs legal and privacy confirmation]:** the case record (reference, methods, outcome, operator ids, timestamps) is kept **24 months** after closure; any retained evidence is deleted **30 days** after closure unless a dispute or investigation requires a hold, which the security lead records. Platform rows follow the life of the account; the audit table is append-only.
   **Deletion of an Auth user (rev5, F6).** The foreign keys from the recovery requests, enrolment windows, approved factors and recovery codes to `auth.users` are `ON DELETE RESTRICT`: deleting a user who has any such history **fails** instead of silently cascading the history away (the audit rows have no foreign key and are append-only either way). Account closure therefore needs a **separate, reviewed erasure procedure** run by two people after the retention period: it is designed as its own SQL revision (never an ad-hoc statement), preserves what the law or an open dispute requires, and records its own audit event. Until that exists, accounts with such history are disabled instead of deleted. The ephemeral rate-limit counters hold only keyed hashes, expire on their own and are not part of this history.
4. **Rights and notices [PROPOSAL]:** the privacy notice for owners mentions operator-assisted recovery and the data used; requests for access or deletion are handled by the project owner with legal advice; a suspected breach of case data follows the incident process and the applicable notification duties.
5. **Verifier hygiene:** work on managed devices, no personal accounts, no public networks for video checks, no recording by participants, screen locked when idle.

## 8. Case references
Format **[PROPOSAL]**: `RCV-YYYYMMDD-NNNN` (letters, digits and hyphen; fits the platform pattern `A-Za-z0-9._:/#-`, 1 to 80 characters). A reference identifies the case in the case system and **contains no personal data** (no names, no company names, no numbers from documents). The initiator's and the approver's references are **different** (each operator keeps their own verification record under the same case); the platform refuses identical references.

## 9. Oversight of operators
The capability is held by the admin role only; every step needs a fresh step-up; quotas limit abuse (5 initiations per operator per 24 hours, 2 per owner per 7 days). The security lead reviews, **monthly**, all `staff.owner_recovery.*`, `staff.owner_mfa_window.*` and `owner.mfa_recovery.*` audit events against the case system (every initiation has a case, two different operators, two references, a notification, an outcome) and reports exceptions to the project owner. Off-boarding removes the capability the same day. Verifiers are trained before first use, retrained yearly, and take part in a yearly drill (a simulated social-engineering attempt).

## 10. What the platform guarantees and what it does not
**Guarantees (rev5, once reviewed, executed and applied):** two different **accounts** with the capability and a fresh step-up, both rechecked as active operators at approval, window creation and the factor approval; per-operator method and case reference recorded; different references; a notarized statement is never a method; the 24-hour cool-off from the second approval; the 72-hour approval deadline enforced by the transition itself; expiry makes a request unusable; the owner's approved factor stays until a window is open and the removal is attested; cancelling or expiring a request revokes its window; owner can cancel; nothing is deleted, and deleting an Auth user cannot silently erase the history; complete audit; no direct MFA bypass. **Quotas apply to committed requests** (a rolled-back call leaves no trace; see `docs/P2-POSTGREST-TX-INVARIANT.md`).
**Does not guarantee:** that the people are different, that the verification was performed or was strong, that the independent channel was independent, that the owner read the notification, that evidence was handled correctly. Those are the duties above and are checked by the monthly review.
