## 1. Decisions implemented
**D-V1 (approved):** an operator-initiated recovery fallback for owners who lost both their authenticator and their recovery codes. Required: **strong external identity verification, two-person authorization, the same 24-hour cool-off, complete audit records, and no direct MFA bypass.**
**D-V2 (approved):** **no silent replacement of an active owner enrolment window.** A second authorization is rejected until the existing window is **explicitly revoked** through an audited operation; **authorization and revocation history are preserved.**

## 2. Design: explicit window revocation (D-V2)
**Behavior change in `rpc_staff_authorize_owner_mfa_window`.** Today the function revokes any open window of the target and inserts a new one (last authorization wins). Rev4: under the existing per-target advisory lock, if the target has a window that is **active** (not consumed, not revoked, `expires_at` in the future) the function returns `window_already_open` (audited as denied, with the id and authorizer of the existing window in the detail, no secret) and changes nothing. A window that has **lapsed** (unconsumed, `expires_at` passed) is not active: it is closed by a **recorded revocation** (`revoked_at`, `revoked_by` = the acting operator, `revoke_reason` = `lapsed`) in the same transaction and audited (`staff.owner_mfa_window.lapse`) before the new window is inserted. The same rule applies to the owner's own additional-factor window in `rpc_owner_open_factor_window` (`window_already_open`; a lapsed own window is closed with a recorded revocation).
**New `rpc_staff_revoke_owner_mfa_window(p_owner_user_id uuid, p_reason text)`.** Gate `private.staff_gate('owner.mfa.authorize', 'staff.owner_mfa_window.revoke', true, ...)` (verified staff session, fresh step-up, dedicated capability); reason 1 to 200 characters; revokes the active (or lapsed) window of that owner; records `revoked_at`, `revoked_by`, `revoke_reason`; **audit event carries the window id, its kind, its original authorizer and its original authorization time** so that both the authorization and the revocation are reconstructible; returns `revoked` or `no_open_window` (audited denied). Cannot revoke a consumed window.
**New `rpc_owner_revoke_factor_window()`.** An owner may revoke only their **own `additional` window** (they opened it); a `first` or `recovery` window is operator-controlled and the owner cannot revoke it (an owner who suspects abuse cancels the recovery request instead). Approved session and step-up required; audited.
**Schema.** `owner_mfa_windows` gains `revoked_by uuid` and `revoke_reason text` (length 1 to 200), with a check that `revoked_at`, `revoked_by` and `revoke_reason` are all null or all not null. The window update guard allows setting these three exactly once together with `revoked_at` and nothing else. **Windows are never deleted by any function** (only by the cascade of deleting the user, whose history stays in the append-only audit table); a consumed or revoked window row is the permanent record. `rpc_owner_claim_factor` is unchanged: a revoked window cannot approve anything.
**Audit actions added:** `staff.owner_mfa_window.revoke`, `staff.owner_mfa_window.lapse`, `owner.mfa_window.revoke`.

## 3. Design: operator-initiated recovery with two-person authorization (D-V1)
**Why it is needed.** In rev3.1 `rpc_staff_authorize_owner_mfa_window` accepts a recovery window only if a **pending recovery request** exists, and only an owner holding a valid unused code can create one. An owner without factor and without codes is stuck.
**Principles.** The fallback must not weaken the primary path and must not be a bypass: it ends in the **same** operator-authorized recovery window, the **same** requirement that the new factor is created inside the window and claimed by the owner with a fresh TOTP, and the **same** 24-hour cool-off. What is added is how a recovery request can come into existence: by two different operators, each recording an identity-verification reference.
**Tables.** `owner_recovery_requests` gains: `origin text not null default 'owner_code' check (origin in ('owner_code', 'operator'))`; `initiated_by uuid`; `verification_method text` (fixed list: `video_id_check`, `in_person`, `notarized_statement`, `verified_business_contact`); `verification_ref text` (1 to 80 characters, a ticket or case reference, never personal data); `second_verification_ref text`; `approved_by uuid`; `approved_at timestamptz`; new status `awaiting_approval`. Constraints: for `origin = 'operator'`: `initiated_by`, `verification_method` and `verification_ref` not null; `approved_by <> initiated_by` whenever `approved_by` is not null; the status `pending` requires `approved_by` and `approved_at`; **`cooloff_until >= coalesce(approved_at, created_at) + 24 hours`** (the cool-off for operator-origin requests is anchored at the **second approval**, not at the initiation, so both persons are committed before the clock starts); an `awaiting_approval` request expires after **72 hours** without the second approval (status `expired`). The partial unique index becomes: at most one request per owner with status `pending` **or** `awaiting_approval`.
**New `rpc_staff_initiate_owner_recovery(p_owner_user_id, p_reason, p_verification_method, p_verification_ref)`** (operator 1). Gate `owner.mfa.authorize` with step-up. Preconditions, each audited as denied when violated: the target is an active owner and **not staff**; factor state is `recovery_required` (history, no live approved factor, **no verified factor at all**: the operator removes factors by hand first, as in the primary path; otherwise the refusal codes are `already_enrolled`, `first_enrolment_applies` for state none, or `unapproved_factor_present`); no pending or awaiting request; no cancellation by the owner in the last 24 hours (`recovery_locked`); the per-operator and per-target rate limits below. Creates the request with `origin = 'operator'`, `status = 'awaiting_approval'`. Audit: initiator, target, method, reference, reason.
**New `rpc_staff_approve_owner_recovery(p_request_id, p_reason, p_second_verification_ref)`** (operator 2). Same gate and step-up. Refuses when the approver **is** the initiator (`self_approval_not_allowed`, audited), when the approver is not an active staff member holding the capability **now**, when the request is not `awaiting_approval` or has expired, and when the target's state changed (a verified factor exists again, or the owner approved a factor). On success sets `approved_by`, `approved_at`, `second_verification_ref`, status `pending`, `cooloff_until = now + 24 hours`, `expires_at = cooloff_until + 7 days`. Audit: both operators, both references.
**New `rpc_staff_cancel_owner_recovery(p_request_id, p_reason)`.** Any operator with the capability, step-up, reason required; cancels an `awaiting_approval` or `pending` operator-origin request; audited. (It does not lock the owner out, unlike an owner's own cancellation.)
**Changes to existing functions.** `rpc_owner_recovery_request`: the check for an existing request counts `awaiting_approval` too (`request_pending`). `rpc_owner_recovery_cancel`: the owner can cancel an `awaiting_approval` or `pending` request of **either** origin (this is the owner's protection against an impersonation attempt on the operators); **any cancellation by the owner, of either origin, starts a 24-hour lock on new requests of either origin** (`recovery_locked`, also enforced by `rpc_staff_initiate_owner_recovery`), so that a thwarted impersonation attempt cannot be retried at once; the cancellation is audited with `origin`. A cancellation by an **operator** starts no lock. `rpc_staff_authorize_owner_mfa_window` (recovery branch): unchanged condition (a `pending` request with an elapsed cool-off), now valid for both origins; the window's `reason` and audit detail record the request id and origin. `rpc_staff_owner_mfa_overview`: adds `origin`, `status`, the two operators' ids and approval time. `rpc_my_owner_context`: `recovery_status` gains `awaiting_approval`; the owner sees that a request exists and can cancel it. The window insert trigger: for kind `recovery` additionally requires, for operator-origin requests, `approved_by` not null and different from `initiated_by`.
**Rate limits (new seed rows, blocking quotas that only constrain operators):** `operator_recovery_initiate` per operator, 5 per 24 hours; `operator_recovery_target` per target owner, 2 initiations per 7 days (limits repeated social-engineering attempts against one owner). Keys derived inside the database from ids (no pepper).
**Audit completeness.** Every step is audited in the same transaction: `staff.owner_recovery.initiate`, `.approve`, `.cancel`, `.deny` variants, `staff.owner_mfa_window.authorize` (with request id and origin), `owner.mfa_factor.approve` (with window kind `recovery`), `owner.mfa_recovery.cancel` (with origin). Detail never contains a secret, hash, token or personal data; verification references are case identifiers only.
**What the database cannot guarantee (state in every report).** That the external identity verification really happened and was strong: the database records the method, the references and the two operators, and enforces that **two different active, capable staff members with fresh step-up** acted and that the **cool-off elapsed**; the quality of the verification is a **procedure** (written policy: acceptable methods, evidence kept outside the database, no verification through the account's own mailbox alone). The owner is notified by the **application** (email), not by the database; the database makes the request visible to the owner in `rpc_my_owner_context` so that the owner can cancel after sign-in. A compromised pair of operators defeats the control (insider collusion): mitigated by audit, the cool-off, owner notification and cancellation.
**No direct MFA bypass (verifiable property).** No new function writes to `auth.*`, approves a factor, creates a session or satisfies `owner_mfa_ok()` or `step_up_ok()`; the self-check that the decision functions contain no reference to recovery is extended to the new request columns and functions. After the window is authorized the owner still has to enrol a factor inside the window and verify it (a fresh TOTP), exactly as in the primary path.

## 5. Tests the revision must come with (offline now, database later)
Static checker (P18 to P20 for the 5 new RPCs), parity of every result code, text-level structural assertions (the `approved_by <> initiated_by` check, the cool-off anchor, the unique index, the absence of `auth.` writes), then the database matrices in `P2-ISOLATED-VERIFICATION-PLAN.md` (groups RV and OR, concurrency CC-25 to CC-30). A rolled-back dry execution on the shared project needs its own approval of the rev4 hash.
